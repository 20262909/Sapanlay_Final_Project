Connectivity, Network Topologies, OSI Model, and TCP/IP

3.1.1. The Internet, Packet Switching, and Network Types

The Internet is a global "network of networks" governed by the TCP/IP protocol suite. Data is split into small addressed chunks called packets that travel independently across routers and are reassembled at the destination via packet switching.

  • PAN (Personal Area Network): Short-range personal device connections (e.g., Bluetooth).
  • LAN (Local Area Network): High-speed network within a single building, classroom, or home.
  • WAN (Wide Area Network): Spans cities, countries, or continents via telecom infrastructure.
  • The Internet: Global public, academic, corporate, and government network ecosystem.

3.1.2. Network Topologies and Hardware

Star & Mesh Topologies

Star: All nodes connect to a central switch/hub (easy to troubleshoot, single point of failure at hub).
Mesh: Redundant multi-node links allow automatic traffic rerouting if one link fails.

Bus & Ring Topologies

Bus: All devices share a single backbone cable (a backbone break halts the segment).
Ring: Closed-loop topology passing data sequentially from node to node.

Interfacing Hardware: Transmission Media (guided copper twisted-pair & fiber-optic cables; unguided Wi-Fi/microwave), Network Switches (route frames/packets to specific local LAN ports), and Routers (gateway devices forwarding packets between distinct networks).
